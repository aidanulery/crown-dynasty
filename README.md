# Crown & Dynasty

A historical strategy game set in fictional 1500s Europe. Rule a kingdom, wage war, marry, raise heirs, colonise the New World, and grow your realm into an empire.

- `index.html` — latest build (currently **pre-alpha v0.2.9**). Open it in a browser.
- `versions/` — every released version, one folder each.
- `src/` — game source (`game.src.html`), map data (`mapdata.js`), town list (`places.json`) and the map generator (`gen.mjs`).
- `tools/` — `build.mjs` (builds `index.html`), `audit-coast.mjs` (coastline audit), `coast-shots.mjs` (coastline screenshots).
- `.claude/agents/coastline-auditor.md` — agent that audits coastlines and tiles on every update.

## Building
```
npm i --no-save d3-geo d3-delaunay topojson-client world-atlas@2 polygon-clipping puppeteer-core
node tools/build.mjs            # index.html from src/
node tools/audit-coast.mjs src/mapdata.js
# tile terrain (plains, forest, mountain, coastal, marsh, swamp) from real data
pip install h5py numpy pillow
node tools/terrain-points.mjs src/mapdata.js tools/.geodata/terrain-points.json
python3 tools/terrain.py          # writes TERRAIN into src/mapdata.js
```

Resources now come only from held land left undeveloped (+1 a tile). Every build (including town development, walls and ships) costs resources. Villages grow each season into market villages and then into new, player-named cities with their own marker on the map. v0.1.0 saves carry over.
- v0.1.2 — farms, fisheries and gold mines must be within two tiles of an owned village or city; a tile can hold one of each of farm, fishery and gold mine, but a village needs a tile to itself; stacked map badges; header notch row no longer clips at mid widths. v0.1.1 saves carry over.
- v0.1.3 — people: capitals 1,000, cities 700 (+150 per development, cost doubling each level), villages 150, market villages 400; villages need an adjacent farm (market villages two) or are abandoned after a season; AI kingdoms start with farms and build farms, villages and mines. Knights notch: three knights (strength, loyalty, intelligence, popularity) rally up to a third of the people as militants, which now decides wars. Romance: foreign suitors (alliance, dowry, claims for children) and noble suitors, courting actions, proposals, a consort with affection, children, succession and regency; foreign rulers age and die, and a claimant of your blood may inherit and unite their kingdom. - v0.1.4 — people die: towns keep a living head count under their capacity; assaults and border skirmishes kill militants and townsfolk on both sides (the loser and a sacked town lose most); famine kills 3–6% of the people each starving season, worsening as it drags on; plague can kill a town's people; fed towns regrow 3% of capacity a season. People counter shows the change per season. v0.1.3 saves carry over.
- v0.1.5 — city view: click one of your cities to open a drawn map of it, with a street plan randomised per city (radiating, grid or winding), coast and harbour for ports, and farmland around. Build village houses (each houses 100; people without homes leave and cities cannot grow past their houses), a castle with four levels (defence; in the capital the consort's affection fades more slowly), and a blacksmith, barracks, market, tavern, mill, chapel and harbour, all for gold. Fortifying a city draws a palisade, stone walls with towers, or great walls with a moat, with gatehouses where roads cross. v0.1.4 saves carry over.
- v0.1.6 — name each royal baby at birth; a newly chartered city takes over the nearby countryside that is now closer to it than to its old city (ties stay with the older city); Treasury notch: tax level (generous, fair, heavy, oppressive), food relief (gold buys food for the people), an emergency levy, and trade agreements with neighbours, allies and port kingdoms (gold every season, and food or resources to buy); war ends trade. Allied kingdoms are fully revealed and our shared border with them is drawn in green. v0.1.5 saves carry over.
- v0.1.7 — 3 deeds a season (2 under a regency). Every build or upgrade on the main map (villages, farms, fisheries, mines, digging mines deeper, raising a market village, chartering a city) now takes a deed; nothing inside a city view does (houses, castle, buildings, developing, fortifying, ships). Fleets: port cities lay down ships at their quays (90 gold, 30 resources, one per season); a port without a harbour moors one ship, a harbour moors eight; one ship carries explorers overseas. Colonies: a harbour city with 5 ships, 300 spare settlers, and 300 gold + 120 resources stored in its warehouses can sail (1 deed) to an unclaimed coast picked on the map, found a named colony that takes the surrounding wild land, and the fleet sails home. v0.1.6 saves carry over.
- v0.1.8 — trade routes: thin white lines joining neighbouring tiles (each tile's line runs from its centre to the shared border, so routes on neighbouring tiles meet). Routes link cities and villages into trade networks; farms, fisheries, mines and wild land send goods to the nearest city or village within two tiles; harbour cities are linked by sea. Only the capital's network fills the royal granary and stores; cut-off networks live on their own food, their towns starve and villages are abandoned if it falls short, and their resources are lost. A road crew costs 1 deed and lays up to 8 segments a season (5 gold each); routes can be abandoned. Treasury page lists each network's food and resources. New colonies plough a first farm. Older saves get routes laid from every city and village to the capital.
- v0.1.9 — every other kingdom is dealt a random power (Feeble, Weak, Middling, Strong, Mighty) that scales its army strength and income, plus a random temper for war. Strong realms start with a more developed capital and extra land; none of the player's starting neighbours is Mighty. Kingdoms expand faster into the wilds, declare war on weaker neighbours more often (mighty ones fight two wars at once), assault towns and conquer whole realms, and make peace after a few years. Wars and conquests between known kingdoms appear under "Abroad" in the season report; the War page shows each realm's power and land. Older saves get powers assigned on load.
- v0.1.10 — other kingdoms follow the player's rules: 3 deeds a season (each build, upgrade, road crew or assault takes one), marches for claiming and occupying land (10 gold per claim), developments cost gold and resources and must lie within two tiles of a village or city, villages need farms and grow into market villages and new cities only when paid for, their own food and resource stores with famine, and their own trade routes (settlements cut off from their capital go hungry). Their in-city work (houses, development, walls) needs no deeds, as for the player. Royal children can be opened on the Romance page to court suitors of their own (letters, gifts, feasts; proposals from age 16; foreign matches seal alliances). The family tree now starts a generation earlier: the late sovereign, the queen mother and any brothers and sisters. Older saves gain these on load.
- v0.2.1 — grandchildren: married royal children have children of their own (named by the player; a daughter's children take their father's house; they inherit claims from both parents), shown in the family tree and on the Romance page; with no living child, the crown passes to the eldest line's eldest grandchild. Look through potential suitors: a list of matches from every known court not at war (with its power) and the nobility, for the sovereign and for each child at any age; invite chosen ones to court (20 gold, up to 6 at once) and send suitors away. Choices in pop-ups now save immediately.
- v0.2.2 — the Family Tree page fills the whole screen below the notches (tree drawn 20% larger on wide screens).

Redraw districts on the map, found new ones, rename them, and appoint a duke or duchess from the royal family or a newly raised noble lord (60 gold) for +1 gold a season per land. Conquered land is hatched with diagonal lines and pays half taxes, yields no resources and cannot be built on until integrated into a district (20 gold a land). Family tree: every card is the same size, cleaner connectors, and a crown marks the sovereign and everyone in the line of succession (numbered); the line is listed under the tree.
